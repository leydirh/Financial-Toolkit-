Tuition

$14,180 / year

The cost for all undergrad programs, according to the U of T website, is $14,180 / year

Transportation

$70 / month

U of T offers their own shuttle bus service that is free for U of T students. To commute around the city, a TTC transit is the most convenient and affordable option. An estimation of the cost will be from the TTC U-pass, which is $70/month. Because the campus is in downtown Toronto, most services can likely be accessible within walking distance.

Food / Housing

$27,120 (Chestnut Dorm)

In the Chestnut residence, the total cost of housing and an unlimited meal plan is $27,120. Dining out would cost ~$80 more. Students online report spending from $200 - $900 for groceries each month. Outside of the campus dorm, rent costs around $1000 / month for rent, along with $100/month for utilities.

Clothing

~$50 / month

Although clothing might not be a monthly expense, occasionally shopping for clothes and shoes are important, as the climate can vary greatly from the seasons of the year. This accounts for ~$50/month.

Phone plans

$50 - $100 / month

Phone plans are $50-100, and can depend on the data usage. Sometimes, there would also be promotional events for students.

Academic supplies

~$1,100 (Fixed cost)

The total cost of textbooks and software is around ~$1100, but that is a fixed cost .

Entertainment & subscriptions

~$200 / month

These include supplies for hobbies, social activities, and subscriptions for streaming services or educational apps. It is estimated to be around ~$200 / month.

Emergency

~$500 buffer

This would act as a buffer if there are urgent needs, such as unpaid bills or medical emergencies. People recommend ~$500.

FINANCIAL WISDOM

Quotations from Credible Sources & Personal Reflections

Reflecting on impactful guidance on saving, borrowing, and budgeting to build sound financial habits.

“Beware of little expenses; a small leak will sink a great ship.”
— Benjamin Franklin
Why this quote is helpful:

This quote is helpful to help me realize that people often pay attention to massive, one time purchases rather than the smaller but regular purchases. In some cases, a habit that consistently requires spending can end up causing a lot more. Expenses such as frequent food deliveries and unused subscription services can drain a student’s budget without them realizing, especially during a time of financial constraints. These small leaks are places where spendings could be reduced, making this quote helpful to realizing that.

“A budget is telling your money where to go, instead of wondering where it went.”
— John C. Maxwell
Why this quote is helpful:

A budget is the way for students to gauge how much money they have and how much they can spend. Students often receive large amounts of money at once, such as OSAP or other scholarships. Without a budget to organize this money, many students overspend at first and have to compensate down the line by borrowing.

STRATEGIC ROADMAP

Action Plan: 3 Steps to Prepare Financially

Three concrete steps to take before entering university to secure financial stability and maximize savings.

1

Save early

RESP & CESG Matching

My first action is to start saving $500 a year in an RESP, which grants benefits through reduced costs for students. This goal can be obtained if I can find a part time job, and if my parents can help me contribute to this as well. Saving early, even by a little bit, can increase the money available by a lot because of compound interest. A RESP offers a 20% direct return through the CESG on the first $2500. The money within the account is also tax deferred, meaning that the taxation is delayed and lowers the tax bracket on which the remaining funds are taxed. I will continue saving in the RESP and spending through it until the end of high school.

2

Create a budget

Pre-University Financial Evaluation

Before entering university, I would make a plan measuring all the expenses that would be needed (listed in the costs above), and how I would be able to balance that with a part time job, monthly allowances, or loans. This is a relevant goal, as having a budget can also decrease the financial pressure during the school year, so more attention can be given to academics. It is also an evaluation into my finances, and how it can be changed to be balanced. This would be done in the year before university, so it can most accurately reflect my financial situation going into college.

3

Look for scholarships

U of T Financial Aid & External Awards

Lastly, I would complete the U of T Need Navigator profile and financial aid application with admission profiles. These scholarships have no impact on my admission, and can range anyway from $500 a year to full scholarships. These scholarships are also offered by companies and organizations, and completing as many scholarships as possible early on ensures that I am considered for major financial aid. This would also be done before applying to university.
